Tokenization Fragmentation

Standard multilingual models shatter Sakha words into subword fragments. We measured avg. pieces/word on 20,000 Sakha tokens for both mBERT and RuBERT, before and after vocabulary extension.

Average subwords per word — 20,000 Sakha tokens
Lower = better. Ideal is 1.0 (no fragmentation). mBERT base produces 3.21 pieces/word — nearly twice the Russian baseline of 2.37.

Key finding: mBERT base fragments Sakha at 3.21 pieces/word (vs. 2.37 for RuBERT, which is pre-trained on Russian text closer to Sakha's geographic context). Vocabulary extension reduces this to 1.46 for mBERT and 1.39 for RuBERT — but better tokenization alone does not guarantee better downstream performance. See the NER section for why.

01

The tokenization paradox

mBERT base has the worst tokenization (3.21 pieces/word, 17.2% single-token) but the best NER F1 (0.535). Vocabulary extension dramatically improves tokenization (→1.46) but degrades NER by 6.1 F1 points. Better subword segmentation alone is not sufficient.

02

The 10% UNK threshold

Extension improved RuBERT (+9.4 F1) when base UNK rate was high (12.25%), but degraded mBERT (−6.1 F1) when coverage was adequate (3.52%). Practical heuristic: measure base UNK rate before extending vocabulary — only extend if >10%.

03

ORG remains the bottleneck

Despite 2× oversampling and active learning, ORG achieved F1=0.232–0.423 vs. 0.361–0.590 for PER and LOC. Only 160 training instances across highly diverse naming patterns proved insufficient for generalization.

Cross-domain asymmetry — exceeds the 4.5pp difference between mBERT and RuBERT. Domain choice matters more than model choice when data is scarce.

Neutral boundary bottleneck: 89% of annotator disagreements cluster at subjective/objective distinctions (49% positive↔neutral, 40% negative↔neutral), not polarity confusions (3%). This mirrors Arabic subjectivity detection findings and confirms neutral-boundary detection is fundamentally harder than polarity classification across morphologically rich languages.

Implications for Typological NLP

These findings directly inform multilingual architectures that rely on subword tokenization for cross-lingual transfer — including systems that derive typological features from POS-tagged text.

Tokenization ≠ task performance

The tokenization paradox shows fragmentation metrics alone do not predict downstream failure. Fragmentation compounds with training data scarcity — the real effect emerges in 1,749-language zero-shot evaluation where agglutinative families have no treebank coverage.

Linguistically motivated segmentation

Vocabulary extension reduces fragmentation from 3.21→1.46. Morphologically aware pre-tokenization (targeting suffix boundaries in agglutinative languages) could stabilize the POS tagger signal before multi-task training — a testable hypothesis under the LMLM research agenda.

Practical decision criterion

Measure base tokenizer UNK rate on the target language before extending vocabularies or relying on cross-lingual transfer. The 10% threshold provides an actionable heuristic generalizable to other Turkic and agglutinative families.
